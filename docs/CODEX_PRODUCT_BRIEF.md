# Busy Does It — public product orientation for Codex

This is a **brief, non-confidential working orientation** for contributors and AI agents. It is **not** the full, owner-approved Word Product Master and must not be described as such. Refer to `docs/CODEX_MASTER_ACCESS.md`.

## Product promise

**"You tell Busy what needs doing. Busy does it."**

Busy Does It aims to be an intelligent, connected operating layer for small businesses. The owner communicates a desired result in everyday language, including typed and voice instructions; Busy prepares appropriate actions, coordinates existing systems, asks permission where required, and confirms what actually happened.

The product should feel extraordinarily simple. Complexity belongs in reusable engineering and responsible automation, not in customers' menus or explanations. Optimize for reliable end-to-end outcomes and customer control over flashy demos or raw feature count.

## Current build-first priorities

**App Builder** and **Website Builder** are foundational. Design for many kinds of businesses and products, not a narrow set of hard-coded sectors.

- The App Builder supports business-facing mini-apps accessible through Busy and a distinct optional standalone export route, according to entitlements and costs. Share brand design, business information, approvals and operational capabilities.
- The Website Builder aims to produce professional, distinctive, responsive and editable websites with limited owner input. Owners can change any supported element in plain language or using direct controls, preserve their work, and control publication and export.
- Coordinate app, website and eventually business content through a single Brand DNA and Business Brain. Prefer real measured/validated improvements, clear uncertainty, private previews, owner approval and reversible changes.
- The owner should not need to understand technical SEO, app infrastructure, marketing campaign settings, integrations or complex back-office software to benefit from Busy.

## Wider platform direction

Connected customer enquiries, CRM, Work/calendar, bookings, quotes/invoices/payments (Get Paid), marketing and discoverability (Get More Customers), content creation, analytics, communication and business operations should increasingly behave like one assistant with reusable foundations. External/provider interactions need clear permissions, cost disclosure and outcome verification.

## Continuous, responsible improvement

- Per-business preferences and memory.
- Structured owner feedback, actual outcomes and evaluation before making recommendations.
- Privacy/consent-first foundations for optional aggregated learning.
- Fair, transparent tier/credit usage and avoidance of surprise charges.
- Ownership, security, reliable backups/undo where appropriate, clear accessibility and inclusive mobile-first interaction.

Avoid claiming a self-retraining model, arbitrary general-purpose app/site generation, deployed capabilities or native release readiness unless supported by actual evidence.

## Engineering implications

Respect tenant/owner security, staged releases, compatible server-client protocols, optimistic concurrency, idempotency, public/private state boundaries, service/provider permissions, and deliberate handling of uncertain or interrupted operations.

Do not create isolated duplicate versions of the same Business Brain, brand, editor, approval, memory, ledger or payment logic when existing modules can be reused or safely extended.

**Owner control:** deploying, publishing, sending messages, taking payments, buying domains or incurring external costs require the right explicit consent and authorisation.

**Evidence:** differentially report supported source behaviour, unit/regression tests, simulation, device tests, hosted staging and production verification. A green offline script is not equivalent to a satisfied customer.
